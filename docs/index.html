How much makerspace time can each programme-year get?

The model packs one makerspace project course per SCI programme-year into room 3203 (50 people, plus the 3202 and 3D-studio side rooms). It covers CTFYS, CTMAT and CFATE years 1–3, COPEN, and 11 masters × 2 years. The page reports supervised daytime hours per project group, and the evening and weekend open access that staffing allows on top. Move the sliders to test the assumptions.

How to read this

Upper bound assumes perfect packing: bookable 3203 hours × parallel sections, shared equally by all sections. Guaranteed and mean come from a timetable simulation. Each programme-year gets a period and a weekly pattern of busy half-days. Its sections are then packed greedily into free 3203 slots. A skilled human scheduler might beat the greedy packing slightly, but never the upper bound.

Bachelor timetables are measured from the VT26 and VT27-draft TimeEdit schedules for CTFYS, CTMAT and CFATE years 1–3 and COPEN, via the published schedule reviews. Years 1–2 have mandatory teaching in about 70% of half-days, and it shifts from week to week, so only 0.7 of 10 half-days are free week after week. A makerspace course must therefore use its own timetable slots. Master timetables and autumn periods are not measured yet.

Open access counts staffed evening and Saturday hours (1 staff + 1 student assistant per open hour, App. E §8.2), times users at once, occupancy and the share used by courses, divided by group members per visit.

Sources. Rooms, limits, equipment and hours come from the SCI MakerSpace Implementation Plan (May 2026), Appendices A, D and E, and the autumn-2026 presentations. Year-1 and external master intake are UHR admission statistics for HT25/HT26. Everything else is an assumption: retention, master show-up rate, internal flow into masters, timetable busyness, occupancy. The browser runs the same model as the Python version (run.py), with fewer random draws, so values can differ by a few tenths of an hour.

One makerspace course per programme-year, academic year 2027/28

The activities on this tab were made up for throughput simulation. The course coordinators have not been involved in this step, and nothing here is a proposal for their courses. Attaching plausible activities to real courses that already have lab or project modules still gives a useful first-order estimate of how much use the makerspace could support.

Each course's picture is split into its project groups. Step through a period, course by course or week by week. Each group then lands as a puzzle piece in the makerspace's slots. Supervised sessions take whole sections of 24 into the course's own daytime half-days. Unsupervised work goes into the staffed evenings, two group members per visit. Change students, group size, hours, period or weeks on any card. Rooms, staffing and section rules are shared with the Capacity model tab and set there. A new seed gives a slightly different year. Bachelor and master thesis groups also use free daytime capacity in P3 and P4.

How the simulation works

Courses: one real course per programme-year from the 2026 programme syllabi on kth.se, mandatory or conditionally elective where possible and with LAB or PRO-type examination modules. The makerspace activities are invented. Participants are summed over the programme-years that take a course; for now each course has one taker with the whole cohort. Many courses are shared between programmes, and Ladok numbers can replace this later.

Daytime: each half-day holds 62 people (3203 plus the 3202 and 3D-studio side rooms). A section of about 24 students in whole groups, plus a teacher, meets only in the half-days its course owns in the timetable. These are taken from TimeEdit for SK1105 and SE1055, and drawn at random per seed for the others (2 for bachelor, 3 for master courses). Hatched cells are booked for other use (23% of half-days, as in the capacity model). Sessions are spread over the course's project weeks, at most 2 per section per week.

Evenings: only staffed evenings are open. Their number follows from the staffing set on the Capacity model tab (staffed hours per week divided by 4 h, at most 6). Each takes as many people as the supervisors on duty can oversee, and a group sends the number of members per visit set there.

Theses: bachelor theses (groups of two) and master theses (individual) in P3 and P4 book free daytime half-days after the courses have been placed, and use evenings when the day is full. Their numbers are assumptions: a share of each year-3 or master year-2 cohort uses the makerspace, with the bench hours shown on their cards. The capacity model has no thesis demand of its own; its "other daytime use" covers part of it. Courses marked "example" are invented but plausible projects. Period dates are approximate.
